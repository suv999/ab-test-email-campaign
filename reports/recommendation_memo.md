# Memo: Which e-mail campaign should we send?

**To:** Marketing Manager · **Re:** Men's vs women's e-mail test (64,000 customers) · **Detail:** [stakeholder summary](stakeholder_summary.md)

## Recommendation: send the men's e-mail to everyone

**Send** the men's e-mail to the whole list. **Don't** split the list by segment, and **don't** skip the send. The
men's e-mail earned the most revenue, and no customer group did better with the women's e-mail.

## Key numbers

*Ranges are 95% confidence intervals: the true value very likely lies within them.*

| | Result | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Extra revenue per 10,000 e-mails, men's e-mail** (2 weeks, vs no e-mail) | **+\$7,700** | \$4,900 – \$10,600 |
| **Extra revenue per 10,000 e-mails, women's e-mail** | +\$4,200 | \$1,700 – \$6,900 |
| **Men's e-mail purchase rate** (vs 0.57% with no e-mail) | **1.25%**, more than double | +0.50 to +0.86 points |
| **Gain from targeting** (women's e-mail to women's-only buyers, men's to the rest), vs men's e-mail to all | **+\$70 per 10,000**, effectively zero | −\$1,800 – +\$1,900 |

**Profitability:** assuming a 40% gross margin, the men's e-mail pays for itself as long as one e-mail costs less
than **\$0.31** (\$0.19 in the worst plausible case).

## The trade-off

- **More buyers, smaller baskets.** The men's e-mail more than doubles purchases, but its median order is
  \$67, compared with \$88 for customers who got no e-mail. The extra buyers still more than make up for the smaller
  orders.
- **The revenue lead over the women's e-mail is real but less precise.** The men's e-mail earns about \$3,500 more
  per 10,000 e-mails (range \$300 – \$6,500). Its lead in visits and buyers is much clearer.
- **One campaign vs tailored campaigns.** The women's e-mail does work on customers who buy women's products, but
  for them it only *ties* with the men's e-mail. Targeting would add work for no measurable gain.

## Limitations

- **2008 US data** from one retailer. Customers and inboxes have changed since then, so treat the figures as
  directional.
- **Two-week window.** Later purchases, unsubscribes and list fatigue were not measured.
- **Unknown e-mail costs and margins.** Profit figures rely on an assumed 40% margin.
- **Segment results are exploratory.** We checked many customer groups, and some differences can appear by chance.
  The "don't target" conclusion should be confirmed in a follow-up test.

## Next steps

1. **Roll out the men's e-mail and keep a 5–10% no-e-mail holdout** in every send, so the effect is measured each
   time.
2. **Run a follow-up targeted test** with three groups, each of about 20,000 customers:
   - the men's e-mail to everyone;
   - matched e-mails (women's to women's-only buyers);
   - a new mixed-assortment e-mail.

   Customers who buy both lines responded most strongly, which suggests broad creative could win.
3. **Bring in real margin and sending-cost data, and track results for 30–90 days,** including unsubscribes, to
   confirm the campaign is profitable over the longer term.
